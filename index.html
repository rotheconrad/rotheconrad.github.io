About

Computational genomics for teams building serious biological infrastructure.

Roth E. Conrad is a Research Scientist with the USDA-ARS Genomics & Bioinformatics Research Unit at the University of Georgia, associated with CAGT and IPBGG. His work sits at the intersection of computational genomics, pangenomics, comparative genomics, scientific AI systems, and open-source bioinformatics infrastructure.

His publication history and early open-source work span microbial evolution, environmental genomics, metagenomics, population genetics, and comparative genomics. That foundation now informs plant pangenomics, graph genomics, long-read sequencing strategy, plant breeding genomics, and AI-assisted scientific workflows. The throughline is systems-level judgment: turning messy biological evidence into reproducible analyses, durable tools, and decisions that research and breeding teams can trust.

Research Focus

Where biological questions meet operational genomics.

Plant Pangenomics & Graph Genomics

Building workflows that make structural and sequence diversity visible, comparable, and useful for plant breeding and germplasm discovery.

Comparative Genomics & Genomic Diversity

Designing comparative analyses that distinguish shared signal, lineage-specific variation, target-private alleles, and candidate genome segments.

Scientific AI and Agentic Workflows

Exploring evidence-aware AI systems that help organize genomic evidence, inspect claims, and accelerate reproducible scientific decision-making.

Genome Assembly Validation and Curation

Supporting long-read assembly strategy, contig review, chimeric contig resolution, scaffolding, dot-plot inspection, and identity validation.

Microbial Evolution and Environmental Genomics Foundation

Bringing a deep background in microbial evolution, metagenomics, population genetics, and comparative genomics into plant-centered pangenome and breeding infrastructure.

Current Work

Breeder-ready genomics with evidence, scale, and reproducibility built in.

Evidence-aware pangenome systems

Developing multi-agent and workflow-oriented systems that can connect genomic evidence, literature, annotations, and breeding-facing questions without losing provenance.

Accession prioritization for long-read sequencing

Creating strategies that estimate the expected marginal contribution of candidate accessions to pangenome diversity, helping teams invest sequencing resources where they add the most information.

Target-private variation and graph segments

Building comparative genomics approaches for finding alleles, markers, and graph segments enriched in target material while filtering background signal.

Reproducible HPC workflows

Operationalizing genomics workflows for real compute environments, with attention to inspectable outputs, failure recovery, traceability, and collaborator handoff.
